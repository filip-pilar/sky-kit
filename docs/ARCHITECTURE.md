# How sky-kit works

## The look

One cloud recipe ("natural cumulus") with painterly shading, in two depth layers:

| Layer | Size | Drift (cloud units/s) | Shape change | Haze |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| far | 0.6 | 0.006 | 0.004 | 0.35, mostly low in the sky |
| near | 1.1 | 0.016 | 0.008 | none |

**Cloud recipe** (`cumulus()`): domain-warped fractal noise plus a little billowy noise, thresholded by coverage. It returns *density* (how much cloud, drives transparency) and *height* (a bump height in cloud units, drives shading). A wide second threshold (`dome`) gives every cloud a rounded overall shape so its inside isn't flat.

**Shading** (cloud fragment shader):
- light from the upper left, a soft two-band ramp between `SHADE` (pastel blue, never grey) and `LIT` (warm white)
- self-shadow: four cheap density lookups toward the sun; only points deep inside, away from the sun, get shaded
- warm glow on sun-facing edges, sky colour bleeding into the far side of edges, faint paper grain
- distant layer fades toward the sky colour

**Cloud units.** Positions are measured in "cloud units": normally one unit = the view height, clamped between `width/2.4` and `0.9 × width` so short banners and tall phones still get sensible cloud sizes (`cloudUnit()`, same formula in both ports).

## Why it is cheap

The cloud shader is expensive (about 40 noise evaluations per pixel, more near a shape). It doesn't run per screen pixel per frame:

1. Each layer keeps **three snapshot textures**, A, B and C, a bit wider than the view (0.3 cloud units of margin each side), at about 1.25 px per CSS pixel (`quality`).
2. A shows the clouds at sky time `t`, B one step later (2 s), both already scrolled to where the view will be at that time.
3. **Every frame** the composite shader only slides A and B and crossfades from A to B over the 2 s. That's 4 texture reads per pixel. The slide offset (`speed × time − snapshot start`) is subtracted on the CPU in double precision, so it stays exact however long the sky runs.
4. Meanwhile C (`t + 4 s`) is painted **one vertical strip per frame**, 24 strips per snapshot, so no frame does a full paint.
5. When time passes B, the snapshots rotate: A ← B ← C, and the old A becomes the new C.

Measured on an M4 at 2880×1800: about 2 ms per frame (vs. 35–40 ms if the cloud shader ran per pixel). With every web extra active, about 3.7 ms at 2560×1600.

The frame rate follows the motion (`frameRate` in both ports): up to `fps` (60), but 30 while nothing moves more than about a point a frame at 30. At speed 1 the clouds drift about 6 points a second on a phone and 14 on a laptop, so they always get 30; a plane crossing a laptop screen (about 54 points a second) or a fast `speed` gets the full rate.

Changing `coverage`, `shadow`, `seed`, `direction` or colours repaints the two snapshots on screen at once (a short hitch: about 24 ms of GPU at 2880×1800 and 10 ms at iPhone resolution on an M4) and the next one a strip per frame as usual; `cloudScale`, `quality` or a resize reallocates them, then does the same.

## Extras (all cheap; same in web and Swift)

- **Planes**: composite-shader overlay between the far and near layers (near clouds pass in front). Two engine trails that merge, widen, wobble, drift with the far layer and fade over `trailLife` seconds. A trail fades in from where its plane started over a length that grows with its width, so an old, wide trail drifting into view never shows a straight cut.
- **Shapes**: text, a built-in symbol (`SKY_SYMBOLS` / `SkySymbol`: the same path data, drawn by the browser and by Core Graphics) or an image becomes a silhouette (100 px tall: the mask is soft anyway, so more pixels buy nothing), turned into four channels and uploaded as one RGBA texture placed in the near layer's *cloud space*, so it drifts with the clouds: R = the shape, from the distance to its outline (`edgeChannel`: the silhouette is smoothed a little so any antialiasing looks alike, the edge is where it crosses ½, found to a fraction of a pixel from the slope, and a distance transform spreads that out; R is ½ on the outline and reaches 1 and 0 a few pixels inside and outside, so every part keeps a solid core however small, which a blur did not: a dot the width of a stroke peaked barely above the edge level and the noise could wipe it out), G = a near halo, B = the shape's body (very wide blur), A = a wide area (a blur about 1.2× the shape's height, faded to exactly zero over the outer 17% of the mask's margin): how near a point is to the shape, used to time a word-to-word morph. The margin is 2.2× the shape's height on every side. The narrower blurs only run on the box they can reach and the wide ones at half or quarter resolution; the build runs off the main thread, so the sky keeps animating while a shape is prepared: in the browser in a Web Worker made from the same functions (`maskChannels`, `pickCloud` and what they call; the worker's source is taken from them, so nothing extra ships and a minified bundle still works), in Swift on a background queue (`SkyController`; `SkyRenderer.showShape` itself builds on the calling thread). Only drawing the silhouette and reading its pixels stays on the main thread (about 5–40 ms on an M4, most of it the first time a page draws text). Where a worker can't start (a Content-Security-Policy without `worker-src blob:`), the build runs on the main thread, yielding between steps. While a shape is on screen, the near clouds over the on-screen part of its area are rendered **live** into a patch (same resolution and cloud function as the snapshots, clipped to the view plus the blend border) and blended in inside the mask's all-zero margin, so there is no seam. The patch keeps its cloud-space position and is placed with the clouds every frame; since its content changes slowly it is repainted at most 30 times per second of sky time, and 15 while the shape only holds (then only the clouds' slow change moves it: measured, a repaint every 1/15 s changes 99.9% of its pixels by at most 2 of 255 levels), and at once after an option change or a resize. The shape only changes the cloud *density* before it is thresholded and shaded, so the shape and the clouds around it are one cloud with one lighting. **Several at once:** each `id` has its own lane: the shape on screen, its queue, its image builds and its mask textures, with the takeover and queue rules below per lane. Up to three lanes are on screen at once; the cloud shader has a slot for each (three textures: mask, cloud map, previous mask) and folds every live shape into the density in turn; a point's tone and rim come from the shape it belongs to most. All the shapes share one live patch over their areas together (repainted at 30 Hz while any forms, morphs or melts, 15 while all hold), so overlapping areas never disagree. Placements run one at a time; a new shape's centre keeps out of the other shapes' silhouette boxes (grown by its own half size) and their source clouds are taken out of its maps, so two shapes never share a cloud. A 4th id takes over the lane used longest ago. With one shape, every render is what it was before lanes existed (the slot math is the same). **Placement:** the shape is put on a real cloud. Maps of the near clouds over everything on screen during the shape's life (0.02 cloud units per cell) are rendered and read back once, for the moments the shape starts forming, holds and ends (in the browser through a pixel buffer and a fence, so the GPU isn't made to catch up, then the cloud is picked in the worker; it's done within a few frames, well before the shape starts forming 0.2 s after the call; `_step` does it all at once; Swift reads them back right away); clouds are told apart in the first one (so clouds that only meet later stay separate), and the others tell how far the chosen cloud reaches later on. `findCloud` (identical in both ports) picks the cloud nearest to the requested `x, y` (strongly preferring the cloud right at that spot when `x` or `y` was given, as for a tap, or when a queued shape follows on the last one's cloud) whose size is closest to the shape's and that best fills a box the shape's size with no other cloud in or right next to it, at a spot where the shape stays in view; the shape is centred on that spot, so it sits on the cloud's mass, not on the empty middle of a ring-shaped one. `sourceCells` (identical too) then makes a map of that cloud, grown a little into where it reaches later on but not into other clouds, fading out 0.05–0.3 units away from the shape's box, so of a big cloud only the part near the shape is used. **Forming** (`form` seconds): the shape grows puff by puff over the forming time (a little sooner where the cloud is), while its cloud thins out evenly, so it shrinks along its own puffy edges (where the map fades out, its edge gets puff-scale noise). Any other cloud the shape touches is only added to, never cut into, so no smooth-edged gaps open next to the letters; such a cloud can merge with the shape where they touch. **Melting** (`fade` seconds) runs it back, so the shape turns into the same cloud again, which stays and keeps drifting and changing like the others; the sky ends exactly as it would have been. Nothing is moved across the sky and nothing is added from elsewhere. `density` (0–1) scales how far the shape's density rises above the cloud threshold below 0.5 (the inside more than the outline, so a low density is wispy and see-through but keeps its shape) and raises it above 0.5, without ever making the noise stronger; and the core of every thin part (strokes, dots: where the wide body blur is low) keeps a minimum density that grows with the setting, so nothing the shape is made of can vanish, while the wide inside of a solid shape may still open up into gaps. The outline's wobble is a few mask pixels, so it scales with the shape and can't tear small parts. `releaseShape()` freezes the forming reached and melts from there (over `fade`, shorter if it had barely formed); `hold: Infinity` holds until then. A shape that has drifted off screen with the clouds ends (nobody sees its cloud come back). `shapeState` reports the phase, label and queue. A shape asked for while another is showing takes over: if that one is holding it turns straight into the new one (as with `pause: 0`); if it is still forming, it does so as soon as it has formed; if it is melting, the new one forms right after it. So a shape never vanishes from one frame to the next. A direct morph starts a millisecond before the current moment, so its first frame already shows it. A blank text or image is ignored (it would only make a cloud fade away). Before the first frame, the web version waits for it. `queue: true` keeps shapes in call order (web: while their images build) and forms each `pause` seconds after the previous one has melted, on the same cloud (`findCloud` strongly prefers the cloud at the previous shape's spot; if it has gone, a cloud near there). **Word to word** (`pause: 0`): once the previous shape has held, the next one takes over at its spot, with its cloud map, and the previous mask stays bound as a second texture. The new shape is present from the start (it doesn't form out of the cloud), and its density blends from the previous shape's to its own (a level-set blend of the two cloud fills), puff by puff over its `form` time, timed by the previous mask's wide blur (A): first where the old shape is, then outward, so a longer word grows out of a shorter one. The live patch covers both shapes' areas meanwhile. The finished shape is built like the other clouds: its outline wobbles at puff scale, its rim and parts of its inside are see-through, its density follows the sky's own unevenness, and its body gets the tone a big cloud's body has (a thin shape barely shades itself; solid ones get less of it, as they shade themselves); thick shapes (a heart, an emoji) also get a low domed body broken up inside into lumps and see-through gaps like a real cloud's, with the outline kept clean enough to read, while thin strokes (text) keep enough density to stay readable. The four self-shadow samples use a cheaper version of the shape (no fine noise). Cost on an M4 at 2560×1600: a word's patch takes about 4 ms of GPU per repaint, so about +2 ms per frame at 60 Hz and +1 ms at 120 Hz while one is showing (more for a big solid shape), nothing otherwise. Changing `direction` clears a shape. In Swift the silhouette comes from CoreText (SF Pro Rounded, heavy) or a `CGImage`, blurred with the same box-blur code (`ShapeMask.swift`); the renderer paints the live patch in `draw(to:)`, just before the composite pass.

## Web ↔ Metal conventions

The Metal shaders (`swift/Sources/SkyKit/Shaders.swift`) are a line-for-line port. Differences handled in the port:

- Metal's fragment position has its origin at the top-left; the shaders convert to GL-style coordinates first: `frag = (pos.x, targetHeight - pos.y)`.
- `dfdy` follows the flipped axis, so its sign is negated.
- Metal texture row 0 is the top, so snapshots are sampled at `v = 1 - y`.
- Fast math is turned off at runtime compile so the noise hash produces the same numbers.
- Parameter blocks are all `float4`, so the Swift and Metal struct layouts match without padding surprises.

Verify after any shader change with `web/examples/parity.html` (see README).
